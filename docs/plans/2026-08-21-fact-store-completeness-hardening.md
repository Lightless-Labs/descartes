## 1. Problem statement

Every baseline/novelty detector in the tree defines "novel" / "anomaly" **relative to fact-history**: an edge, entity, or value is flagged when it is *not present* in the fact-store window. That verdict implicitly asserts the history is **complete** — that if the thing were normal it would already be on file. It is not complete, and the incompleteness is today **silent**.

Three silent-loss channels exist in `fact-store.js`, none of which survives to the detector that later reads the store:

1. **Retention scrubs corruption before any detector reads it.** `enforceFactRetention` calls `readJsonLines`, which drops unparseable lines and returns their count as `corrupt_count`; the function surfaces it as `corrupt_dropped_count` in its *return value* but **persists nothing**. It then rewrites `facts.jsonl` (tmp+rename) with only the parseable, in-window records. The corrupt lines are now permanently gone. The next `readFactPoints` sees a clean, shorter file and reports `corrupt_count: 0`. A detector that keys cold-start off "did I personally observe corruption this tick" (which is exactly what `process-lineage-baseline.js:321`, `enteringColdStart = factsCorruptThisTick || storeLossThisTick`, does) trusts a truncated history in full, one tick after the truncation it never saw.

2. **`readFactPoints` drops schema-invalid-but-parseable records with ZERO signal.** At `fact-store.js:174-181` each record is re-run through `normalizeFactPoint` inside a `try{}catch{ return undefined }` and silently filtered out. These lines parse as JSON, so they are **not** in `corrupt_count` — they vanish with no counter at all. A truncated/garbled write that still yields valid JSON is invisible.

3. **Byte-cap eviction of in-window ticks is indistinguishable from age-eviction.** `enforceFactRetention` (`:125-136`) walks newest-first and stops when `usedBytes + size > maxBytes`, dropping the **oldest** records even if they are inside every detector's active window. A novelty detector cannot tell "this edge was genuinely never seen" from "the tick that would have shown this edge as long-established was byte-cap-evicted." Age-eviction (records older than `retentionMs`) is expected and benign; byte-cap eviction of not-yet-aged records is a completeness hazard — and the two are today the same undifferentiated `dropped_count`.

**Net effect:** a perfectly normal edge/entity/value can read as "never seen" and **fabricate** a novel-edge / anomaly alert whenever history was shortened by corruption, a garbled-but-parseable write, or byte-cap pressure. That is a direct violation of the never-fabricate-a-security-signal invariant, reached not by inventing a satisfied constraint but by trusting an incomplete substrate.

Each detector closes a *narrower* version of this one layer up, in its own baseline store (persistent cold-start on **its own** store loss — see `process-lineage-baseline.js:294-356`). But the underlying **fact-history** loss is a **shared, unsignalled exposure** that no per-detector store can observe, because the loss happens in the shared substrate before the detector runs.

### 1a. The inherent host-local limit (stated honestly, up front)

A **root-capable local attacker** who rewrites `facts.jsonl` into a crafted, schema-valid, internally-consistent store — and rewrites any completeness sidecar this plan adds to match — cannot be caught by anything that lives on the same host, because every artifact is the attacker's own file. **This plan does not claim to solve that.** Its goal is narrower and honest: make **accidental / corruption / retention-driven** incompleteness **observable and fail-closed**, and record continuity metadata that *raises the cost and visibility* of tampering without pretending to prevent it. The root-adversary case is explicitly delegated to the off-host attestation / fleet dead-man's-switch layer (`todos/2026-08-11-tamper-evidence-attestation-design.md`, fleet topology plan). The Safety Analysis (§6) states this boundary as a first-class limit, not a footnote.

### 1b. Why now

The shipped `process-lineage` detector and the **planned persistence + credential-file-access detectors** (`docs/plans/2026-08-11-agent-intrusion-detection-gaps-impl.md`) are precisely the high-consequence novelty detectors that fabricate hardest on a truncated store: a "first-ever cron/LaunchAgent persistence entry" or "first-ever access to `~/.ssh`/credential file" alert is *only* meaningful if the absence of prior history is real. Landing those detectors on top of a substrate with three silent-loss channels bakes the fabrication risk into the most security-sensitive alerts in the system. The substrate *should* be hardened **before** those detectors ship — this plan is *intended* as the prerequisite the intrusion-gaps plan depends on, but that sequencing is this plan's own claim, not yet ratified by the operator nor stated in the intrusion-gaps plan itself. Whether it is a hard blocker or a documented, shippable limitation (as `process-lineage` itself shipped, per §1a/1b precedent) is Open Decision 5 (§7) — flagged here rather than asserted. The gap was surfaced by `gpt-5.6-sol` during the process-lineage adversarial review (see HANDOFF 2026-08-12 session update) and escalated to a todo rather than patched in circles precisely because it is cross-cutting.

## 2. Design direction — a durable completeness/continuity contract

Introduce a **durable, central completeness signal** at the fact-store layer, analogous to the per-tick census marker but scoped to the store's own history integrity, and decide its trust semantics **once, centrally**, so every detector inherits the decision instead of re-implementing per-store cold-start against a signal it cannot see.

**Representation: a sidecar integrity ledger, not in-band facts.** A companion file `learned/facts/integrity.json` (0600, atomic tmp+rename, sibling of `facts.jsonl`) holds **counts and timestamps only — never fact contents, never identifiers**:

- `generation` — id minted on first creation (P8 bootstrap).
- `schema_version`.
- **Lifetime monotonic totals — audit/telemetry only, never read directly by any trust decision** (see the Addendum below on why): `corrupt_dropped_total`, `schema_invalid_dropped_total`, `bytecap_evicted_total` (kept **distinct** from benign age-eviction), `age_evicted_total` (benign aging; never a fail-closed trigger by itself). These feed Open Decision 2 (cap sizing) and the CLI surfacing in §2d, not `completeness.status`.
- **Per-channel most-recent-event timestamps — this is what status/trust actually compare against**: `last_corrupt_ts`, `last_schema_invalid_ts`, `last_bytecap_evict_ts`, `last_continuity_break_ts`. Each is overwritten (not accumulated) to the timestamp of the most recent occurrence of that channel's loss event.
- `first_degraded_ts` — lifetime audit only: the timestamp of the very first loss event of any kind ever recorded for this store. **Never clears, and is never read by `completeness.status` or `factHistoryTrustworthy`** — it exists purely so an operator/CLI view can say "this store has never been perfectly clean since X." See Addendum below.
- `continuity`: `{ record_count_hwm, oldest_ts, last_rewrite_record_count, last_rewrite_newest_ts, last_rewrite_bytes, last_committed_pass_id, continuity_ok }` — the wholesale-truncation cross-check and its reconciliation bookkeeping (see §2a, rewritten).

An **in-band** marker (a reserved `fact.store.census` fact inside `facts.jsonl`, mirroring the other census markers) was rejected: the marker would live in the very file being retained/corrupted, could be byte-cap-evicted or garbled exactly when it is most needed, and would conflate history with meta-history. The sidecar is written transactionally with each retention rewrite by the one function (`enforceFactRetention`) that already computes every drop count.

> **Addendum 2026-08-21 (adversarial gate, must-fix — recovery latch, must-fix — ledger write-path/counting):** The original schema derived `degraded` from the lifetime monotonic totals (`corrupt_dropped_total > 0`, etc.), which never decrease — one corrupt line, ever, in the store's entire life made `degraded` permanent, and per the persisted-lockout semantics this plan adopts (§2c), re-arming on every subsequent tick discards re-accumulation progress, so re-establishment could never complete. This is now fixed structurally: the lifetime totals are demoted to audit/telemetry-only fields, and the four `last_*_ts` fields (new: `last_schema_invalid_ts`, `last_continuity_break_ts`; existing two kept) are what `completeness.status` and `factHistoryTrustworthy` actually evaluate, compared against a time boundary (a caller-supplied anchor, or the read's own window — see §2b/§2c). A loss event that predates the relevant boundary no longer withholds trust, by construction, without needing a separate "un-latch" mechanism.
>
> `first_degraded_ts` and each detector's persisted `cold_start_since_ts` (§2c, §4 Slices 3–8) are two **different** fields with two different, now-explicit semantics: `first_degraded_ts` is ledger-level, lifetime, audit-only, and intentionally never clears (it answers "has this store ever been dirty," not "should I suppress right now"). `cold_start_since_ts` is per-detector, is intentionally a monotonically-advancing high-water mark that also never clears to `undefined` even after a lockout resolves (this is correct, not a bug — it is the comparison anchor future loss events are checked against, and clearing it would make the detector unable to distinguish "already handled" from "brand new" the next time a loss event occurs). Only `cold_start_pending` (the boolean) toggles on recovery; the anchor timestamp does not need to. See §2c for the full mechanism and the required recovery test.
>
> The ledger's write-path and counting discipline are also now pinned, closing the read-path-mutates / retention-double-counts ambiguity: (1) `readFactPoints` **never writes** the ledger — its per-read `schema_invalid_count` is report-only, this-read-only, and does not touch `integrity.json`; a read-only CLI/detector call stays read-only. (2) `enforceFactRetention` is the **single** ledger writer. (3) Retention gains schema-validation via `normalizeFactPoint` (it currently keeps any parseable record — `fact-store.js:117-134` — regardless of schema validity); records that parse as JSON but fail `normalizeFactPoint` (including an unparseable/invalid `ts`, today silently folded into undifferentiated `dropped_count` by the bare `Number.isFinite(tsMs)` filter) are now classified `schema_invalid`, not age-evicted, and each such record increments `schema_invalid_dropped_total` **exactly once** — this is an intentional behavior change from today's "keep any parseable record" retention behavior, with its own test (Slice 1). (4) A test asserts no double-counting across two consecutive retention passes over an unchanged output file (retention's own rewrite already removes classified-invalid records, so a second pass over untouched output finds nothing new to drop by construction; the one path that could double-count is the crash-between-writes case in §2a, which is handled there via `last_committed_pass_id`).

### 2a. Continuity cross-check (catches out-of-band truncation)

> **Addendum 2026-08-21 (adversarial gate, must-fix — continuity cross-check unimplementable):** the original spec relied on `record_count_hwm`/`append_hwm` alone. A live record count below the HWM is the **normal** outcome of benign age/bytecap eviction, so an HWM-only check cannot distinguish that from out-of-band truncation — it either never fires (leaving the fabrication path §2a exists to close still open) or fires on every benign eviction (compounding the recovery-latch bug just fixed in §2). Rewritten below to pin the exact fields, the invariant they rely on, and the reconciliation rule at each retention pass, including the crash-window case P2 already flags but never specified a detection mechanism for.

**Pinned invariant:** `facts.jsonl` only grows between retention passes. Appends (`appendFactPoints`) are append-only; the *only* place records are ever removed is inside a retention rewrite. So between two successive **committed** retention passes, the live record count can only increase (via appends) — it can never legitimately decrease outside a rewrite.

**Pinned fields** (in `continuity`, §2): `last_rewrite_record_count` and `last_rewrite_newest_ts` — the record count and newest-record timestamp of the **output** of the last successfully **committed** retention rewrite (i.e., the state of `facts.jsonl` immediately after the tmp+rename that ended that pass); `last_rewrite_bytes` — its byte size, for supplementary corroboration; `last_committed_pass_id` — a monotonically incrementing id, advanced only when a pass's facts rename is confirmed to have committed (see reconciliation below); `record_count_hwm`/`oldest_ts` — retained as lifetime audit fields, not used by the reconciliation rule itself.

**Reconciliation, run at the start of every retention pass, before computing this pass's drops:**
1. Read the live `facts.jsonl` record count (`liveCount`, from the same `readJsonLines` call retention already makes).
2. If `liveCount < last_rewrite_record_count` — the file is **shorter** than the last committed rewrite's own output, with no rewrite having run in between to explain the shrink (a rewrite would have updated `last_rewrite_record_count` to match its own output before this pass ever saw it). There is no legitimate way for that to happen short of an out-of-band `> facts.jsonl`, partial disk loss, or a stale-backup restore. ⇒ **`continuity_ok: false`**, and `last_continuity_break_ts` is set to `options.now` (this pass's injected clock, P7).
3. If `liveCount > last_rewrite_record_count` but the excess is **larger** than can be explained by ordinary appends since the last committed pass — practically: this pass is simply the *next* pass, appends happened normally in between, and this is the expected, benign case — reconciliation is **ok**, proceed to classify+rewrite as normal.
4. **Crash-window case (P2):** the ledger is written *before* the facts rename commits (per P2's ordering). If a crash lands between those two writes, on the *next* invocation the ledger already reflects an assumed-committed rewrite (`last_rewrite_record_count` was already advanced to the *intended* post-rewrite count) but the live `facts.jsonl` is still the **pre-rewrite** (longer) file — i.e. `liveCount` is larger than what a file already truncated to `last_rewrite_record_count` would show, in a way that doesn't reconcile against `last_committed_pass_id` (the pass id in the ledger doesn't match a facts file whose own accounting the retention function can re-derive as already-applied). This is the "ledger's accounting is ahead of what the facts file confirms" case ⇒ **`continuity: unknown`** for this read (never `intact`, never `broken` — a crash is not evidence of tampering). **Correction of the inflated counters:** because retention re-reads the *live, still-unrewritten* file fresh on this next invocation and re-runs the *same* classification deterministically over the *same unchanged* input, it reproduces the *same* pass (same drop set, same counts) rather than computing a *new* one — the fix is that the monotonic total increments for a pass are only treated as final once `last_committed_pass_id` is confirmed to match a facts rename that actually landed; a pass whose facts rename didn't confirm is **superseded, not added to**, by the retry of that same pass on the next invocation. No new increment is applied for records already accounted for by an unconfirmed prior ledger write.
5. If the ledger is **missing or unreadable/corrupt**, continuity is **unknown** (degrade-not-fabricate: an unprovable history is not an intact one; this mirrors the canary collector's `read_ok` distinguishing genuinely-empty from unreadable, HANDOFF 2026-08-12).

**Named tests (Slice 1):** benign eviction across a pass never flips `continuity_ok` to `false`; an out-of-band truncation between passes (test harness shrinks `facts.jsonl` directly, bypassing retention) flips it to `false`; a simulated crash *between* the ledger write and the facts rename (both orderings: crash-after-ledger-before-rename, and — as a negative control confirming the chosen ordering is the safer one — crash-after-rename-before-ledger) resolves to `unknown`, never `intact`, never a false `broken`; two consecutive passes over the identical unchanged output produce identical ledger totals (no double count); a missing/corrupt ledger resolves to `unknown`.

This cross-check is deliberately weak against a root adversary (who edits both files and can forge `last_committed_pass_id` consistently) and deliberately strong against accidents and non-root/out-of-band truncation — which is the honest scope (§1a).

> **Addendum 2026-08-21 (round-2 adversarial IMPL-review, `gpt-5.6-sol` — CONTENT DIGEST added; the earlier deferral is OVERRIDDEN).** The original plan chose count+newest+`last_rewrite_bytes` for continuity and deferred a content digest to "avoid hashing on every read." Two things invalidate that deferral, surfaced by the impl-level adversarial re-review: (1) **it left a real, non-root hole** — an equal-length record *replacement* (`entity_key:"nginx"` → `"mysql"`, same byte length, same newest_ts) and a *replacement-plus-append* both read `intact`, because count/bytes/newest all still matched. Any process running as the user (not just root) can do that edit, so it is squarely inside the "strong against non-root out-of-band" scope this section claims. (2) **The stated cost does not exist** — `readFactPoints` already reads the entire `facts.jsonl` into memory to parse it, so hashing those same bytes is marginal, not a new I/O pass. Therefore continuity is now **content-bound**: `enforceFactRetention` stores `continuity.output_digest = sha256(canonical committed bytes)` (and the intended-output digest inside `pending_pass`); a read verifies the committed **prefix** (first `last_rewrite_bytes` bytes of the live file) hashes to `output_digest`, treating any mismatch as `broken` and allowing only an appended tail beyond the committed prefix. **Privacy:** a digest is a fixed-size fingerprint, neither content nor an identifier, over facts whose identities are already hashed-at-source — so it does **not** widen the "counts and timestamps only" contract in substance; the contract is revised to "counts, timestamps, and content digests only." **Threat-model effect:** the digest moves equal-size/prefix-preserving tampering from *trivially undetectable by any non-root process* to *requires forging the sidecar digest too* — i.e. it pushes the attack up to the §1a root-adversary boundary (must rewrite both `facts.jsonl` and `integrity.json` coherently), which this plan already concedes is out of on-host scope. It does **not** claim to beat local root. The digest also hardens the crash-recovery path (§2/§2c): continuity may recover `null → true` only when the retry's committed digest **equals** the pending digest — an unconditional recovery would otherwise launder a *changed* retry (crash, then facts truncated before retry) into `intact`. Finally, **every** `ledger === null` reason (missing / invalid / unreadable) now bootstraps into persisted `unknown` requiring a later independent clean pass — an unreadable ledger is more suspicious than a missing one, never less. See the round-1 review `sol-substrate-review.md` (6 HIGH) and round-2 `sol-substrate-rereview.md` (4 CLOSED, then #4 + N1/N2/N3) for the full audit trail.

### 2b. The contract `readFactPoints` exposes to detectors

`readFactPoints` gains **additive** return fields (existing `points` and `corrupt_count` unchanged, so every current consumer keeps compiling and passing before any of them is hardened):

```
{
  points,                      // unchanged
  corrupt_count,               // unchanged: THIS read's JSON.parse failures
  schema_invalid_count,        // NEW: THIS read's parseable-but-invalid drops (was silent)
  completeness: {               // NEW: durable, from the sidecar ledger
    status: "intact" | "degraded" | "unknown",   // window- or lifetime-scoped, see below
    // Operational fields -- what status/trust are actually computed from (§2 Addendum):
    last_corrupt_ts, last_schema_invalid_ts, last_bytecap_evict_ts, last_continuity_break_ts,
    // Audit/telemetry fields -- never fed into status/trust directly (§2 Addendum):
    corrupt_dropped_total, schema_invalid_dropped_total, bytecap_evicted_total, age_evicted_total,
    first_degraded_ts,          // lifetime, audit-only, never clears -- see §2 Addendum
    continuity_ok               // bool | null(unknown) -- last retention pass's standing verdict
  }
}
```

`status` derivation (degrade-not-fabricate — the *safe* default is the pessimistic one), evaluated against a boundary timestamp `asOf`:

- `asOf` defaults to the read's own window start (`options.now - options.windowMs`, the same cutoff `readFactPoints` already computes to filter `points`) when the caller passes `windowMs`; with no `windowMs` (a whole-history/audit read, e.g. the CLI status surfacing in §2d), `asOf` is unbounded (lifetime).
- `unknown` if the ledger is missing/unreadable/corrupt, OR continuity could not be evaluated this pass (§2a case 4).
- `degraded` if any of `last_corrupt_ts`, `last_schema_invalid_ts`, `last_bytecap_evict_ts`, `last_continuity_break_ts` falls at or after `asOf`. `continuity_ok` is also surfaced verbatim (§2d) as the last retention pass's standing verdict, but the *trigger* for `degraded` is the timestamp comparison above, not a separate un-windowed boolean — this keeps all four loss channels under one mechanism.
- `intact` if none of the above hold.
- Age-eviction alone (`age_evicted_total`) never downgrades below `intact` — it has no `last_*_ts` channel feeding `degraded`.

This `completeness.status` (window- or lifetime-scoped, as above) is what a **stateless** caller (no persisted anchor of its own — incident-correlation, §4 Slice 8; CLI/audit reads, §2d) uses directly. A caller with its **own** persisted re-establishment state does not use this pre-scoped `status` for its trust decision — it uses the raw `last_*_ts` fields via `factHistoryTrustworthy`'s `anchorTs`, below, because its own anchor is a tighter, tick-precise boundary than a multi-week read window (see the recovery-latch fix in §2, and §2c).

> **Addendum 2026-08-21 (adversarial gate, must-fix — recovery latch):** replaces lifetime-total-based derivation (`corrupt_dropped_total > 0`, etc.) with the boundary-timestamp comparison above. See §2 Addendum for why lifetime totals could never un-latch, and §2c for how each detector supplies the tighter anchor its own re-establishment mechanism needs.

### 2c. The central trust decision (decided once)

A single pure helper — `factHistoryTrustworthy(readResult, opts)` in a new `src/fact-store-completeness.js` — folds the read result into one verdict every detector consumes:

```
trust = (corrupt_count === 0)
     && (schema_invalid_count === 0)
     && (completeness.status !== "unknown")
     && !hasLossEventAfter(completeness, opts.anchorTs)
```

`opts.anchorTs` (optional ISO timestamp | undefined) is the boundary a loss event must be *after* to count against trust — the detector's own "already accounted for, up to here" watermark:

- **Callers with a persisted lockout** (§4 Slices 3–8, every detector except incident-correlation) pass their own persisted `cold_start_since_ts` as `anchorTs`, **on every call, whether currently pending or not** — this is the *same* field process-lineage already maintains (`process-lineage-baseline.js:283-356`) as a monotonically-advancing high-water mark that is set to `options.now` when a lockout (re-)arms and is **never reset to `undefined`** even after the lockout resolves (this is intentional, not the bug the "align since_ts semantics" fix flagged — see §2 Addendum). A loss event dated at-or-before the detector's own `cold_start_since_ts` is one it has already locked for and re-established past; only a loss event *strictly after* that watermark is new information and re-arms. When `persistedState.cold_start_since_ts` is `undefined` (a brand-new or freshly-migrated store), `anchorTs` is treated as `-Infinity` — any recorded loss event counts, which is moot in practice because such a store already starts `cold_start_pending: true` (P8; see §4 Slices 3–8).
- **Stateless callers** (`opts.anchorTs` omitted) fall back to the read's own window — i.e. this reduces to reading `completeness.status` as computed in §2b. This is the correct, and only safe, choice for incident-correlation (§4 Slice 8), which has no persisted re-establishment state of its own to anchor against, and for CLI/audit callers.
- `hasLossEventAfter` is the single place this comparison is implemented — no detector re-derives timestamp arithmetic.

Returns `{ trust: bool, reason }` where `reason ∈ {corrupt_facts_this_tick, schema_invalid_this_tick, history_degraded, history_unknown, ok}`. Detectors do **not** re-derive this; they call the helper and feed `!trust` into their existing persistent-cold-start lockout (the mechanism already built in `process-lineage-baseline.js:321-356` — enter a persisted `cold_start_pending`, emit zero novelty claims, and require `minHistoryTickCount` genuinely-new complete ticks to re-establish). For process-lineage, the only change is **what arms the lockout**: today it is `factsCorruptThisTick` (this-tick only); after this plan it is `factsCorruptThisTick || storeLossThisTick || !factHistoryTrustworthy(readResult, { anchorTs: persistedState.cold_start_since_ts })`, which additionally catches the durable/degraded/unknown cases the detector could never see before. For the other five detectors, the lockout mechanism itself (the `cold_start_pending`/`_reason`/`_since_ts` fields and the arm/re-accumulate/clear state machine) does not exist yet and must be built — see §4 Slices 4–8, which is the substance of must-fix 1 below.

> **Addendum 2026-08-21 (adversarial gate, must-fix — recovery latch, required test):** every slice that adopts this mechanism (§4 Slices 3–8) must include this exact test: *given a single transient loss event (one corrupt line, or one bytecap eviction, or one continuity break) followed by `minHistoryTickCount` genuinely-new, complete, clean ticks (using `options.now` to simulate elapsed time — no real sleep, per P7), the detector's `cold_start_pending` clears and novelty detection resumes.* This is the literal "nothing ever un-degrades" bug's regression test. A second required test per slice: *a loss channel that recurs faster than `minHistoryTickCount` ticks can accumulate (e.g. bytecap eviction on every append, under sustained fact-rate pressure at the 5MB cap) never clears — this is a **true, defined, and — per must-fix 4 below — operator-visible** steady state, not a silent unrecoverable blackout.* This second test is the honest acknowledgment that recovery is bounded, not guaranteed-fast: it directly informs Open Decision 2 (§7, cap sizing).

> **Addendum 2026-08-21 (adversarial gate, should-fix — malformed-input handling):** `factHistoryTrustworthy` must never throw and must never optimistically trust a shape it doesn't recognize. Pinned as an explicit Slice 2 test row: called with a `readResult` missing the `completeness` field entirely (an unhardened caller, or a test fake built to the pre-Slice-1 shape) returns `{ trust: false, reason: "history_unknown" }` — the same "any doubt ⇒ `trust: false`" rule stated for `status`, made explicit here because every detector accepts an injectable `options.readFactPoints` and a stub/mock is exactly the kind of caller that could hand the helper an old-shape result.

### 2d. Minimum observability (v1 requirement, not an open decision)

> **Addendum 2026-08-21 (adversarial gate, must-fix — silent global suppression):** the original plan framed the behavioral effect of degraded/unknown history as strictly-more-suppression that "can only reduce fabricated alerts," as if suppression were free, and deferred any operator-visible signal to Open Decision 1 ("start silent"). It is not free: degraded/unknown history mutes **every** novelty detector at once, and both trigger channels are influenceable by a non-root actor — crash or fill-disk mid-write for corruption, fact-flooding via many sessions/processes/peers to force bytecap eviction under the 5MB cap. A detection blackout with no operator-visible signal is a mute button: an attacker induces degradation, does the novel thing during the blackout, and no one ever learns detection was off for that window. The repo's own established posture is the opposite of silent-suppression here — the canary collector ships tamper-**aware** (`canary.tampered`, not silent suppression) for exactly this reason. "Fail-closed but fail-silent" contradicts the other half of degrade-not-fabricate: the operator must be able to see that the system currently cannot vouch for novelty. This is now folded in as a v1 requirement, split into a mandatory part and a still-open part:

**Mandatory (v1, this plan, no further ratification needed):** store completeness status is surfaced deterministically in existing **read-only** CLI output — `descartes learned status [--json]` (`runLearnedConfigCommand`, `src/constraint-store.js`) gains a `fact_store_completeness` section reporting, per fact-store instance: `status` (`intact`/`degraded`/`unknown`, lifetime-scoped per §2b), the four lifetime totals, `first_degraded_ts`, and `continuity_ok`. This is counts/timestamps only — the exact shape the ledger already restricts itself to (§2) — so it carries zero identity and zero fabrication risk; it reports only what the ledger recorded, verbatim. This closes the "attacker induces degraded to mute detection, and no one ever finds out" gap: an operator running the existing status command sees it. No new alert namespace, no new gate, no LLM path — a straightforward read of an existing read-only surface (P4/P9 unaffected).

**Still open (§7 Open Decision 1, unchanged in spirit but now explicitly scoped as *additional to*, not *instead of*, the mandatory CLI surfacing above):** whether a degraded-transition should *also* emit a deterministic, non-LLM, fail-closed-namespaced counts-only signal mirroring the `budget_exhausted` pattern (`src/alert-intelligence.js`) for push-style visibility, versus relying on an operator to periodically pull `learned status`. The CLI surfacing above does not require this decision either way.

Added to §6 Safety Analysis as an analyzed trade-off with the mitigation named (not merely asserted safe).

## 6. Safety Analysis (invariant-by-invariant)

- **Read-only by default / no mutating action without a gate.** The substrate emits no alerts and takes no system action; it only annotates internal bookkeeping. The *behavioral* effect on detectors is strictly **more** suppression (cold-start), never more firing — it can only reduce fabricated alerts, never authorize an action. No policy/authority gate is bypassed or added. **Addendum 2026-08-21 (adversarial gate, must-fix — suppression is not free):** this bullet previously stopped at "more suppression can only reduce fabricated alerts," treating the suppression itself as costless. It is not: see the new bullet below — a detection blackout is itself a security-relevant cost, even though it is never a *mutating* one.
- **Degrade-not-fabricate.** This is the plan's entire purpose. Every ambiguous state resolves pessimistically: missing ledger ⇒ `unknown`; unreadable ledger ⇒ `unknown` (not a throw, not `intact`); facts/ledger desync ⇒ `unknown`; any corruption/bytecap loss *within the relevant boundary* (the caller's anchor or read window, §2b/§2c — not a lifetime total, per the recovery-latch fix) ⇒ `degraded`. `intact` is reachable **only** by positive proof (clean ledger + continuity + zero loss within the boundary). A degraded/unknown store yields **no** novelty verdict — the detector emits cold-start, i.e. "unknown", exactly as the invariant demands, rather than inventing a "novel" or asserting "known-good".
- **Detection blackout is a security cost, not a free side effect, and must be observable (must-fix, adversarial gate 2026-08-21).** Degraded/unknown history mutes **every** novelty detector at once. Both trigger channels are influenceable by a non-root actor: crash or fill-disk mid-write induces corruption; fact-flooding via many sessions/processes/peers forces bytecap eviction under the 5MB default cap (`DEFAULT_FACT_MAX_BYTES`). A blackout with no operator-visible signal is a mute button — an attacker induces degradation, does the novel thing during the window, and no one learns detection was off. This is analyzed, not merely asserted safe: (1) the blackout is **bounded**, not permanent, per the §2/§2c recovery-latch fix — an attacker cannot mute detection forever with one event, only for the time it takes `minHistoryTickCount` clean ticks (or, for incident-correlation, the read window) to elapse after their last induced loss event; sustained, repeated induction (faster than that bound) is the one case where suppression is a true steady state, and that case is exactly the one made operator-visible next. (2) Minimum observability is a v1 requirement (§2d), not deferred: `descartes learned status --json` surfaces store completeness (counts/timestamps only, zero identity, zero fabrication risk — it reports only what the ledger already recorded) so an operator pulling status can see "detection has been degraded since T" even though nothing pushed them a notification. (3) The push-notification form of this signal (mirroring `budget_exhausted`) remains genuinely open (§7 Open Decision 1) — its absence in v1 is a real, named gap (an operator who never runs `learned status` during the blackout window still misses it), not a solved problem, and is stated as such rather than glossed over.
- **Identity hashed/bucketed at source; never store/emit raw identifiers.** The integrity ledger stores **counts, timestamps, a generation id, and high-water integers ONLY**. It MUST NOT copy any `entity_key`, `attributes`, `fact_name`, or fact content into itself — doing so would re-introduce identity at rest outside the domain-hashed fact schema. Slice 1's adversarial-verify explicitly greps the written ledger for identifier leakage; this is a hard test gate, not a guideline.
- **Fail-closed alert namespaces / kill-switch.** The ledger lives under `learned/facts`, inside the subsystem gated by `learned.json` (default OFF). It has no entry point outside the existing gated append/read/retention paths and is inert when the subsystem is disabled (P4). No alert namespace is added; no namespace's fail-closed posture is weakened. The §2d/P9 CLI surfacing (`descartes learned status --json`) is the one new code path this plan adds; it is read-only against whatever ledger/facts files happen to exist (none, when the subsystem has never been enabled — `fact_store_completeness` degrades to `unknown`/absent exactly like any other missing-file read in this codebase) and does not itself gate on `learned.json`, matching `runLearnedConfigCommand`'s existing behavior of allowing status/enable/disable to be checked regardless of current enablement — it emits no alert and takes no action, so it does not create a new fail-closed namespace to weaken.
- **No new LLM path.** Pure `fs` + arithmetic. Nothing here reaches the opt-in/rate-limited/audited/`enableTools:false` wakeup, and nothing narrows toward it. The only LLM path is untouched.
- **No new execFile surface.** No process is spawned; no shell; no argv. The invariant is trivially upheld and stated so a reviewer confirms the absence is intentional.
- **Explicit host-local limit (honest scope boundary).** A root adversary who rewrites both `facts.jsonl` and `integrity.json` consistently defeats the on-host cross-check. This plan does not claim otherwise (§1a). That case is owned by off-host attestation / fleet dead-man's-switch (`todos/2026-08-11-tamper-evidence-attestation-design.md`, fleet topology plan). The continuity cross-check (§2a) still raises the bar against non-root, out-of-band, and accidental loss — which is the achievable, honest win.
- **Concurrent-writer limit (N4 — DEFERRED, `todos/2026-08-23-fact-store-concurrent-writer-locking.md`).** `gpt-5.6-sol`'s round-3 impl-review found that two *concurrent* writers to the store (P1 snapshots `A`; P2 appends `B`, retains, finalizes; P1 renames stale `A` over `A+B` and writes its aligned stale ledger) can lose acknowledged history and still read `intact` — the digest cross-check (§2a) does not catch it because P1's stale digest matches its own stale output. This is **deferred, not fixed here**, on three grounds stated honestly: (1) **pre-existing** — the original committed `fact-store.js` already did tmp+rename with no lock, so this work surfaces the race, it did not introduce it; (2) **reachable only via a two-daemon misconfiguration** (sol round-4 correction of an earlier, wrong "unreachable" claim) — `enforceFactRetention` has exactly one caller (`appendFactPoints`), wired from the single site `daemon.js:520`, and a single daemon serializes its own ticks; but **nothing enforces single-instance**, so the installed service (`daemon run --foreground`, `daemon.js:678`/`:692`) plus an independently-started `descartes daemon run --foreground[ --once]` (`daemon.js:1039`) against the same state dir can both reach the append and race. There is no concurrent *CLI* append path today, so it is not reachable in normal single-instance operation — but it is not the impossibility first claimed; (3) **cross-cutting** — the correct fix is a store-wide interprocess lock (or a single-instance daemon guard) that also protects `history-store.js`/`shadow-store.js`/`constraint-store.js`, orthogonal to completeness. The follow-up todo records the reachability triggers (second daemon instance; a CLI verb that appends concurrently; a second `enforceFactRetention` caller) and the fix sketch (per-store `O_EXCL`/`flock` around the whole transaction + snapshot re-verify before rename + per-operation temp names + a two-writer barrier test). Until then this is a **named, bounded limitation**, not a silent gap.

## 7. Open decisions for the operator

> **Addendum 2026-08-21 (adversarial gate):** Decision 1 is narrowed — the CLI (pull) surfacing it used to gate is now a v1 requirement (§2d/P9), not open; only the *push*-notification form remains open. Decision 3 is updated for the recovery-latch fix (§2/§2c) — recovery is no longer "the only escape hatch is a full re-accumulation cold-start with no time bound," it is bounded per the new mechanism, which changes what "acceptable" means here. Decision 5 is new (should-fix — §1b sequencing was previously asserted, not decided).

1. **Byte-cap eviction of in-window ticks — degrade-and-cold-start only, or also a *push* operator notification?** This plan treats bytecap eviction as a `degraded`/cold-start trigger, and — as of this revision — a **pull**-visible one (`descartes learned status --json`, §2d, v1, not optional). What remains open is whether it should *additionally* push a dedicated low-severity notification ("fact-store under byte pressure; history completeness reduced"), mirroring `budget_exhausted`, since sustained bytecap eviction is an operational signal (store too small for the fact rate) as well as a completeness hazard and an operator who never polls `learned status` during a sustained-eviction window (§2c Addendum's "true steady state" case) still misses it. Recommendation: start without the push signal (CLI pull is the v1 floor, not the whole answer); add the push notification in a follow-on if bytecap eviction proves common enough that reliance on polling is impractical. Operator to confirm.
2. **`DEFAULT_FACT_MAX_BYTES` (5MB) adequacy.** If bytecap eviction of not-yet-aged ticks is frequent under the current 30-day / 5MB envelope, the substrate will keep detectors in cold-start more than desired, and — per §2c's sustained-bytecap steady-state test — a store under continuous eviction pressure will *never* clear degraded for the affected channel, by design (a true, not latched, steady state). Do we raise the cap, or accept this as the safe default? (Deferred until Slice 1 telemetry — the new `bytecap_evicted_total` counter and the now-`degraded`-since-visible-in-`learned status` signal are exactly the measurement needed to decide.)
3. **Ledger recovery UX.** With the recovery-latch fix (§2/§2c), a genuinely benign continuity break (e.g. an operator legitimately restored a backup) is no longer a permanent block: it resolves via the same bounded, anchor-based re-accumulation every other loss channel uses — `minHistoryTickCount` clean ticks past the detector's own `cold_start_since_ts` (or, for the shared ledger's own `continuity_ok` field, past the next successful retention pass reconciling it, §2a). What remains open is only whether that bounded wait is acceptable UX for a *known-benign, operator-initiated* event (a legitimate restore) versus wanting an explicit "acknowledge + reseed `generation`" affordance to skip the wait entirely. Recommendation: none for v1 — the bounded cold-start is now genuinely bounded and cheap (not the open-ended latch the pre-fix design had), so there is less pressure for a manual override; revisit only if the bound proves noisy in practice.
4. **Does the `intact`-only-fires policy risk under-alerting during the upgrade migration window (P8)?** Every existing install cold-starts once on upgrade. Confirm this one-time, bounded suppression across `minHistoryTickCount` ticks is acceptable (it is the safe direction — the alternative fabricates on a store whose completeness was never tracked).
5. **Does this plan hard-block the persistence/credential-file-access detectors (§1b), or may they ship with the fact-store completeness gap documented as a known limitation, the way `process-lineage` itself shipped with its own follow-on-needed gaps?** §1b currently states this plan "is intended as" that prerequisite (softened from an unqualified claim — should-fix, adversarial gate 2026-08-21) but the sequencing has not been ratified by the operator, and the intrusion-gaps plan does not itself state a dependency on this one. Operator to confirm which posture applies before Slice 10 closes this plan out.
